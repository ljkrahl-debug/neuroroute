# Worked example — "Füllstand" (Neuroroute launch film V2)

24 s, 9:16, 48 beats at 120 BPM, drawn and scored entirely in code. The
founder's reaction: "Ich war noch nie so zufrieden mit dem Ergebnis." After
V2.1 (more effects, stronger sound): "Ey ich find das mega." This is the bar.

Neuroroute is a logistics marketplace: shippers post transports, carriers
offer them; its two ideas are *partners first* (your own network gets a 24 h
head start) and *empty runs become revenue* (a truck that drives back empty
is offered on a marketplace with one click).

## The idea

**Füllstand — fill level: filled = loaded, hollow = empty.** One line carries
the whole film. The outbound trip is a filled tube; the return trip is the
same tube, hollow. Type follows the same rule (solid words vs. outlined words),
and so does the UI (a hollow edge = free capacity). Every scene grows out of
the previous one; there is not a single cut or crossfade.

Why it worked: the rule is learnt in two seconds and then pays off — the
hollow tube *filling up* is the product's promise, shown, not said.

Colours carried meaning: cyan = the brand / active, amber only for the empty
leg, green only for "empty run turned into revenue".

## V2.1 — what "more effects" turned into

The founder's feedback on V2: the opening loop (empty back) and the ending
(full back) were boring; the cards in the middle were good but could have
effects on clicks; "the circle — make more of it"; the sound did not carry.

- **Opening**: camera starts close on the station and travels with the glowing
  head of the line (comet, sparks). Full words slam in (big, motion-blurred,
  white flash); empty words trace themselves like a flickering neon tube. At
  the end the camera flies back into the station — the loop stays seamless.
- **Payoff**: the liquid rises with bubbles and a glowing front, the surface
  inside the letters sloshes, it splashes over at the top.
- **Hits**: shockwaves through the frame (impact, every click, drop, market
  opening, logo); colour split on the hardest hits and the whips; speed lines
  in the whips.
- **Cards**: panels swing in in 3D; the slider fires a tick per percent; booking
  and accepting send an energy pulse round the frame.
- **The circle (network)**: comes in tilted and rotated and swings to the front;
  the 24 h run as a radar with a light wedge and a sonar ping per beat; when it
  opens, a shockwave runs outwards and every node flashes.

## Sound

- Music: 120 BPM, A minor. Heartbeat intro, groove from the card on, build on E
  (the dominant) with a snare roll and a 1/16 of silence, drop on A minor, logo
  on Am(add9). Braam and 808 carry the drop.
- Picture-driven: the line zips with its speed; the hollow return whistles like
  wind through an empty tube; empty words knock hollow, full ones thud; the
  slider clicks once per percent exactly when the knob passes it; the liquid
  rises in pitch like a filling bottle; 24 clock ticks while the ring runs the
  24 hours; whips peak at the camera's fastest frame.
- Recorded layer (Mixkit): each effect placed by its measured peak, swells
  capped, three tiers — drop, market opening and logo loudest; whips and
  transitions below; clicks and UI quiet.
- Mix: music ducked by effects, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dB. Measured: the drop
  is the loudest moment; the build before it is quieter.

## Process that led there

1. Two TikTok motion-design references downloaded and inspected as 2 fps frame
   sheets; principles written down (continuous transformation, one camera,
   type as image, sound on every move), not their content.
2. Concept + beat map + four keyframe stills shown before the full build.
3. The founder allowed UI drawn in code instead of screenshots — it can morph.
   Every feature shown was checked against the backend (the 24 h partner
   window applies to transport requests, not marketplace listings, so they are
   two separate chapters). Prices and company names came from the prototype's
   demo data.
4. Contact sheets and transition strips each round; pop scan, seam and loudness
   checks on every render.

## What to take from it for any product

- Find the product's *before → after* and make it a property of one object
  (empty → full, scattered → connected, slow → instant, red → green, many → one).
- Let type and UI obey the same rule as the object.
- The payoff is the moment the rule flips, on the drop, with the biggest
  effects and the loudest sound.
- End where you started so it loops.
